How we appraise

A worked example: six units, West London

Every deal we present is built on the same appraisal discipline. Here is that process applied to a hypothetical West London scheme, so you can see how we arrive at a number — and how the fee calculation works.

Illustrative example. These assumed figures demonstrate our appraisal method. This is not a completed transaction, an available property or a projection of returns on a deal we may source for you.

The brief

Assume a portfolio builder is looking for a West London commercial-to-residential scheme, targeting a minimum 25% return on cost, with capital in place and a twelve-month horizon.

The site

Assume a tired retail unit with vacant upper floors on a W3 side street, with poor frontage and a dated layout. The concept is six flats and a modest rear extension, subject to planning, design and technical checks. No planning entitlement is assumed to be confirmed.

What our fee would be

For this illustration only, assume the agreed fee basis is return on cost before Kestrel’s fee. At 25.9%, the 25–29.9% tier gives a 4% finder’s fee on the £680,000 purchase price.

Finder’s fee: £27,200

The £1,000 non-refundable reservation fee forms part of that fee, leaving £26,200 payable on exchange for a private investor, or on completion for a corporate investor, under the sourcing agreement.

Adding the £27,200 fee raises the illustrated cost to £1,925,950 and reduces profit to £464,050: 24.1% on cost and 19.4% on GDV. On these assumptions, the return after our fee falls below the brief’s 25% target and would need to be addressed before proceeding.

The ROI definition and fee basis are agreed in writing before sourcing. This example does not set those terms for your deal.

This simplified illustration is not a complete investment appraisal. Selling costs and any additional tax or VAT treatment are not modelled; these would need to be established and included where applicable. Values, costs, finance, timing and planning are assumptions, not independently verified evidence for this example.

How we would evidence a live appraisal

  • Sold comparables — completed sales of equivalent units within a defined radius, adjusted for size, floor and condition.
  • Build costs — rates benchmarked against current local tender returns, with the specification and scope made clear.
  • Conservative assumptions — values at the lower end of the supported range and costs tested against downside scenarios.
  • Contingency held, not spent — an allowance retained against the unknowns that conversions can produce, sized to the actual scheme’s risk.

Every figure that sets our fee is evidenced in the deal pack before you commit.
